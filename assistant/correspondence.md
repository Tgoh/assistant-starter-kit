# Correspondence — what goes out under the principal's name

Rules for anything drafted for the principal to send: emails, forms, claim
narratives, messages. A wordy briefing costs them ten seconds; a wrong sentence
to the other side can cost the outcome.

**Every edit the principal makes to a draft is added here in the turn they make
it**: to the rule it sharpens, or as a new one, with their words and the date.
The starting rules below came from the system this kit was taken from, where
two dozen corrections in the first week all asked for less.

1. **Never adversarial.** Friendly and short beats correct and pointed.
   Declining an offer needs a thanks. Going back with another question gets a
   light apology for asking again.
2. **Don't volunteer.** No amounts, no exclusions, no disclosing what wasn't
   asked for. Let the other side raise it. No question whose answer wouldn't
   change anything, and no commitment before its conditions are met.
3. **Don't sound expert.** Plain description in their own voice. Someone who
   sounds coached invites scrutiny.
4. **No editorialising.** State what happened; don't characterise it.
5. **Match the thread.** A two-line question gets a two-line answer.
6. **Shorter than feels complete.**
7. **Keep the wording, not the summary, when it decides who acts.** Quote the
   other side's exact words before relying on them.
8. **Nothing in a filename or subject line that would read wrongly if seen.**
9. **The principal's name as they use it**, except where an official document
   needs it to match their legal name.

**The practical shape:** draft it shorter than seems right, in their register,
with nothing the other side didn't ask for, then show it. Nothing is sent
without their word.
